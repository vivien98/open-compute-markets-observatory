## The strongest parts of the argument

The concentration argument is strongest when it emphasizes complementarity and lead times. AI capacity is a system, bottlenecks move, and a high willingness to pay can shift rents upstream without instant physical expansion. It is also right that cash accounting, income accounting, and financing commitments must be separated.

The explanation of pre-contracted capacity and credit is especially valuable: a customer commitment can make a project financeable, while a balance-sheet-rich builder can preserve the option to sell later at scarcity prices.

## The most fragile parts

The most fragile steps are:

- extrapolating very rapid revenue-per-MW growth;
- treating GW as a stable unit across hardware generations and workloads;
- assuming two labs remain persistently ahead in model quality and monetization;
- assuming regulation constrains external use more than internal research in a particular way;
- translating trillions of AI credit into a near one-for-one rate increase for other borrowers;
- projecting explosive aggregate growth and equating it with equally explosive interest rates;
- moving from a stressed sovereign-debt mechanism to specific country defaults without country-by-country balance-sheet analysis;
- reading a quoted token price as a paid price, when the cached leg is unverifiable and the router ranks on the wrong number.

## A better forecasting discipline

Build three scenarios rather than one point forecast:

**Capacity-constrained boom:** model revenue rises fast, supply remains slow, compute prices and supplier margins rise, and debt issuance expands.

**Competitive normalization:** algorithms and supply improve, model and token prices fall, utilization grows, but revenue per MW stabilizes. More capacity is built without extreme scarcity rents.

**Overbuild/reset:** commitments outrun profitable demand, model differentiation narrows, financing tightens, projects are delayed, and older hardware or specialized sites are impaired.

For each scenario, write explicit paths for delivered GW, useful FLOPs per watt, utilization, revenue per useful FLOP, installed cost, OpEx, financing mix, and policy.
